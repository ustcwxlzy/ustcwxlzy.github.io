项目 — Projects

The first two entries describe two parts of the system reported in Optics Express (2026). Results are publication-reported measurements under the stated conditions; this review did not independently reproduce the experiments.

MEMS Closed-Loop Beam Acquisition & Tracking

The published system combines a two-axis MEMS mirror, PMT optical feedback, FPGA/XADC acquisition, and SPI control. Rotating raster scanning, sliding-window detection, and spatial clustering support coarse acquisition; nutation scanning and Kalman-based prediction support fine tracking.

  • Minimum mean acquisition time reported: approximately 2.01 s; acquisition statistics use 42 target positions, one trial per position for each configuration.
  • Reported bench tracking errors: approximately ±10 mm at 0.25 m/s and ±15 mm at 0.50 m/s, using a motorized one-dimensional stage and reference trajectory.

No dedicated active beacon is used; a cooperating passive retro-reflective target is still required. Bench tracking results should not be read as underwater positioning accuracy.

Source: Optics Express (2026) · Individual responsibilities remain to be confirmed.

Publication-reported

Real-Time Underwater Optical Communication Terminal

The same system integrates waterproof optical terminals, a Zynq-7100 transmitter/controller, and a Zynq-7020 receiver for frame synchronization, data recovery, and real-time BER calculation. Pool experiments use a fixed transmitter and an ROV-mounted receiver.

  • Reported rate–distance operating points: 10 Mbps over 50 m, 50 Mbps over approximately 25 m, and 100 Mbps over 20 m.
  • PRBS transmission and BER measurements evaluate active tracking against a manually aligned baseline in relatively clear swimming-pool water.

These are separate tested operating points, not a 100 Mbps / 50 m result, application throughput, or open-ocean validation. See the paper for the measurement protocol and BER criterion; these are reported experimental results, not guaranteed operating limits.
